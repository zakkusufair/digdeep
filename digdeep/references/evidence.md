# Evidence gaps and saved witnesses

Read the relevant section when the conclusion depends on provenance, a causal
claim, unread material, or the boundaries of saved quotes and summaries. These
checks do not require a separate report, every possible source or a fixed route.

## Origin and causal claims

- Follow the originating account, experiment or record when it could change the
  decision. Preserve author, date/revision and relevant conditions. Distinguish
  a source's own observation from an attributed account and our inference.
- Articles and reposts referring to one case form one origin chain. Keep useful
  corrections, contrary observations and added conditions; another domain alone
  is not an independent observation.
- Verify separately what was reported, what the accessible material shows and
  what supports the proposed cause. Post metadata establish provenance of a
  report; they do not establish the reported event's truth. A general capability
  or policy does not establish its use in a particular case, and a later outcome
  alone does not identify its cause.
- For a measured result, inspect the original method, baseline, inputs,
  denominator and version as relevant. Repetition of the result does not fill
  missing measurement conditions. An independent reproduction can add evidence.
- An inaccessible original can leave a useful, attributed account. Attempt an
  applicable permitted route, retain the precise gap and
  unlock condition if it remains, and continue useful independent branches.
  Do not invent missing facts or keep retrying an unchanged failure.
- Signals that a passage needs its origin checked before it supports a claim:
  future or conditional wording (could, may, plans to, is expected), unnamed
  sources or passive attribution, general qualifiers without specifics, an
  aggregator or retelling instead of the original, and a proposed design or
  hypothetical example placed beside measured results. These prompt verification;
  they are not grounds to discard a lead.

## Match the representation to the missing content

| Missing evidence | Applicable representation when available | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Source identity, author, date or linked origin | Relevant page metadata, public API/feed, exact title/ID or attributed originating link | Truth of the report or unseen attachment content |
| Text or visual evidence inside an attachment | Actual accessible media, relevant frames/OCR, or a clearly attributed lawful copy/export with its identity checked | Cause of the event or all omitted media |
| Measurement or transfer conditions | Original protocol, documentation, code/data or the author's detailed account | Independent reproduction or untested local applicability |

Apply the access-recovery rules in SKILL.md to the content needed. A login page,
navigation wrapper, CDN link, HTTP success or empty parser is not that content.
Use an available independently permitted representation; do not bypass denied
access or assume that a named browser/API is available. For example, when a wrapper
exposes a usable direct media URL, try it for decision-relevant missing content if
permitted; check the media's identity and needed content.
Apply the task-scope and stopping criteria in SKILL.md. A branch may pause when
permitted routes or required authorization are exhausted, or a user/host limit
is reached. An irrelevant attachment or a gap that cannot change the current
decision needs no further recovery. State a material unread part where it limits
a claim and the conditions for revisiting it.

## Example for a requested evidence handoff

This is a schematic format, not retrieved evidence or a mandatory database:

```text
SOURCE: exact URL and relevant section/revision/date
QUOTE (verbatim excerpt): "..." [Relevant omissions explicitly marked.]
SUMMARY (researcher): Paraphrase or explanation outside the quoted span.
SUPPORTS: The specific part of the claim supported by the passage.
LIMIT: A material part that remains unverified, when relevant.
```

A mixed file may contain both quotes and summaries when their boundaries are
explicit. Reserve an "unchanged original response" label for the unedited return,
and label excerpts as excerpts. A screenshot can preserve visible text without
establishing that the claim in that text is true. Link the actual source used,
copying its URL/revision from retrieved material or metadata. Preserve relevant
redirects and regional identity; a normalized or replacement URL needs identity
and content support before it is called verified.
This example does not require saving every response or creating extra files.
